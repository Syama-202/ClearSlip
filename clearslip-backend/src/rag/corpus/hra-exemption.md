## What HRA is
House Rent Allowance (HRA) is a salary component paid by the employer to help with rent. It is fully taxable unless you live in rented accommodation and claim the exemption under section 10(13A). The exemption is available only under the old tax regime.

## How the HRA exemption is calculated
Under Rule 2A, the exempt amount of HRA is the least of three figures: (1) the actual HRA received; (2) rent paid minus 10% of salary; (3) 50% of salary if you live in a metro city, or 40% of salary elsewhere. For this calculation, "salary" means basic salary plus dearness allowance (DA) that counts for retirement benefits. Any HRA above the exempt amount is taxable.

## Which cities count as metro
For HRA, only four cities count as metro cities and get the 50% limit: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. All other cities, including Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune, use the 40% limit.

## Proof needed to claim HRA
Submit rent receipts and the rental agreement to your employer during the proof-submission window, usually between January and March. If the annual rent is more than ₹1,00,000, you must give the landlord's PAN to the employer. If you miss the employer's deadline, you can still claim the HRA exemption when filing your income tax return under the old regime.

## Paying rent to parents
You can claim HRA for rent paid to your parents if you actually pay the rent, ideally through a bank transfer with a rental agreement, and the parent owns the house. Your parent must show the rent as income in their own tax return. Rent paid to a spouse is generally not accepted for HRA.

## HRA when you don't pay rent
If you live in your own house or with family without paying rent, the whole HRA is taxable. It is still part of your salary; there is simply no exemption.
