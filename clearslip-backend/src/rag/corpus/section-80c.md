## The ₹1.5 lakh limit
Section 80C lets you deduct up to ₹1,50,000 a year from taxable income for specified investments and payments. The limit is a combined total for all 80C items. Section 80C is available only under the old tax regime.

## What counts under 80C
Common 80C items are: your own EPF contribution (the PF deducted from your salary), voluntary provident fund (VPF), Public Provident Fund (PPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS mutual funds), life insurance premiums, 5-year tax-saver fixed deposits, National Savings Certificates (NSC), Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, children's tuition fees (up to two children), and the principal part of a home-loan EMI.

## How much 80C space is left after PF
Because your employee PF contribution already counts under 80C, subtract the yearly PF deducted from your salary from ₹1,50,000 to see how much more you can invest. For example, if ₹3,000 PF is deducted every month (₹36,000 a year), you can invest another ₹1,14,000 to use the full limit.

## Lock-in periods
ELSS funds have the shortest lock-in among 80C options at 3 years. Tax-saver fixed deposits are locked for 5 years, and PPF has a 15-year term with partial withdrawals allowed after some years.

## Declaring 80C to your employer
Declare planned 80C investments to your employer at the start of the year so less TDS is deducted, and submit proofs in the proof-submission window (usually January to March). This only matters if you have chosen the old regime.
