## CTC, gross salary and take-home pay
Cost to Company (CTC) is the total yearly amount the employer spends on you: gross salary plus employer contributions such as employer PF, gratuity provision and insurance. Gross salary is the total of all earnings before deductions. Take-home (net) pay is gross salary minus deductions such as employee PF, professional tax, ESI and TDS. That is why take-home is noticeably lower than CTC ÷ 12.

## Basic salary
Basic salary is the core fixed part of pay, usually 40–50% of CTC. PF, gratuity and HRA are calculated as a percentage of basic, so a higher basic means more retirement savings but lower take-home.

## Dearness Allowance (DA)
Dearness Allowance is an inflation-linked allowance, common in government and public-sector jobs. It is fully taxable and is added to basic when calculating PF, HRA exemption and gratuity.

## Special allowance
Special allowance is a flexible component employers use to balance the CTC after other components. It is fully taxable in both regimes.

## Conveyance and medical allowance
Fixed conveyance (transport) allowance and medical allowance paid monthly are fully taxable. Their old exemptions were replaced by the standard deduction from FY 2018-19. Reimbursements of actual official expenses, backed by bills, are treated differently from fixed allowances.

## Bonus and variable pay
Bonuses, incentives and variable pay are fully taxable in the year they are received. Employers usually raise TDS in the month a bonus is paid.
