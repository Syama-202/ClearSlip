## Telling your employer which regime to use
At the start of the financial year (or when you join), your employer asks which tax regime you want so it can calculate monthly TDS. If you choose nothing, the employer uses the new regime, which is the default. The choice made with the employer only affects TDS during the year.

## Switching regimes every year
A salaried individual who has no income from business or profession can pick either regime every year at the time of filing the income tax return, regardless of what was told to the employer. If too much tax was deducted, the excess is refunded after the return is processed. People with business income can switch back to the old regime only once.

## How to compare the two regimes
To compare, calculate tax both ways: under the new regime, subtract the ₹75,000 standard deduction (and any employer NPS contribution) from gross salary and apply the new slabs; under the old regime, subtract the ₹50,000 standard deduction plus all eligible deductions such as HRA exemption, 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D and home-loan interest, then apply the old slabs. Add 4% cess to both. The regime with the lower total tax is better for that year.

## Rule of thumb for first jobs
For a salary up to ₹12,75,000 the new regime gives zero tax, so the old regime cannot beat it. Above that, the old regime only wins if total deductions are large — typically several lakh rupees through HRA, 80C, NPS and a home loan together.
