## Who uses the new regime
The new tax regime under section 115BAC is the default regime for individuals from FY 2023-24 onwards. If a salaried employee does not tell their employer which regime they want, the employer deducts TDS using the new regime. A salaried person with no business income can still choose the old regime each year when filing the income tax return.

## Tax slabs for FY 2025-26
Under the new regime for FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), income tax is charged in slabs: up to ₹4,00,000 nil; ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000 at 5%; ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 at 10%; ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000 at 15%; ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000 at 20%; ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000 at 25%; and above ₹24,00,000 at 30%. These slabs were introduced by the Finance Act 2025.

## Standard deduction
Salaried employees get a flat standard deduction of ₹75,000 from salary income under the new regime. It is given automatically; no proof or investment is needed. Pensioners get the same deduction.

## Rebate under section 87A — zero tax up to ₹12 lakh
Under the new regime, a resident individual whose taxable income does not exceed ₹12,00,000 gets a rebate under section 87A of up to ₹60,000, which makes the tax nil. With the ₹75,000 standard deduction, a salaried person earning up to ₹12,75,000 a year pays no income tax. The rebate does not apply to income taxed at special rates, such as certain capital gains.

## Marginal relief just above ₹12 lakh
If taxable income is only slightly above ₹12,00,000, marginal relief applies: the tax payable cannot be more than the income earned above ₹12,00,000. For example, with taxable income of ₹12,10,000 the slab tax would be ₹61,500, but marginal relief limits it to ₹10,000 (plus cess).

## Health and education cess
A health and education cess of 4% is added to the income tax amount after rebate. Surcharge applies only when income exceeds ₹50 lakh.

## Deductions not allowed in the new regime
The new regime gives lower slab rates but removes most exemptions and deductions. Not allowed: HRA exemption, leave travel allowance (LTA) exemption, section 80C, section 80CCD(1B), section 80D, professional tax deduction, and interest on a home loan for a self-occupied house. Still allowed: the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the employer's contribution to NPS under section 80CCD(2).
