## How TDS on salary works
Under section 192, your employer must deduct income tax from your salary every month. The employer estimates your total taxable income for the whole financial year, calculates the tax under the regime you chose, and spreads it over the remaining months. This monthly deduction is shown as "TDS" or "Income Tax" on your payslip.

## Why TDS changes during the year
TDS can change because of salary revisions, bonuses, or investment proofs. If you declare investments at the start of the year but do not submit proofs by the employer's deadline (often January to March), the employer recalculates tax without those deductions, and TDS in the last months — especially February and March — can jump sharply.

## Declaring investments: Form 12BB
Under the old regime, employees declare planned investments and rent through Form 12BB (or the employer's portal) so that TDS is lower during the year. Under the new regime most deductions do not apply, so there is little to declare.

## Checking TDS was deposited
Tax deducted by your employer should appear against your PAN in Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS), available on the income tax e-filing portal. Check these before filing your return.

## Getting back excess TDS
If more tax was deducted than you owe — for example because you claimed deductions only at filing time — you get the excess back as a refund after you file your income tax return and it is processed.
