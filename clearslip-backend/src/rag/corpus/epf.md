## Why PF is deducted from salary
The Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a mandatory retirement savings scheme run by EPFO. The "PF" deduction on your payslip is your own contribution, normally 12% of basic salary plus dearness allowance. It is not a tax — the money goes into your own PF account, earns interest and is yours.

## Employer's PF contribution
Your employer also contributes 12% of basic plus DA. Of this, 8.33% goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), calculated on wages up to ₹15,000 a month, so at most ₹1,250 a month goes to EPS; the rest goes to your EPF account. The employer also pays small charges for the EDLI insurance scheme. The employer's share is usually not shown as a deduction because it is paid on top of your salary, but it is included in your CTC.

## UAN and checking your PF balance
Every member gets a Universal Account Number (UAN) that stays the same across jobs. You can see your PF balance and passbook on the EPFO member portal or the UMANG app after activating your UAN. When you change jobs, keep the same UAN so the account can be transferred.

## PF interest rate
EPFO declares the EPF interest rate every year. For FY 2024-25 the rate was 8.25%. Interest is credited to the account once a year.

## Tax treatment of PF
Your own PF contribution qualifies for deduction under section 80C in the old regime. Interest on PF is tax-free, except interest on your own contributions above ₹2,50,000 a year, which is taxable. Withdrawals after five years of continuous service are generally tax-free.

## Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)
You can ask your employer to deduct more than 12% as Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF). VPF earns the same interest as EPF and counts under 80C in the old regime, but the employer does not match it.
