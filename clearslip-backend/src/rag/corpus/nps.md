## What NPS is
The National Pension System (NPS) is a government-regulated retirement scheme. You can contribute yourself, and some employers also contribute to your NPS account as part of your salary package. Contributions build a retirement corpus; part can be withdrawn at 60 and part is used to buy an annuity.

## Your own contribution: 80CCD(1) and 80CCD(1B)
Your own NPS contribution can be deducted under section 80CCD(1), but this shares the ₹1,50,000 limit of section 80C. On top of that, section 80CCD(1B) gives an extra deduction of up to ₹50,000 for your own NPS contribution, beyond the 80C limit. Both are available only under the old tax regime.

## Employer's contribution: 80CCD(2)
When your employer contributes to your NPS account, that amount is deductible under section 80CCD(2) in both regimes. Under the new regime, the deduction is allowed up to 14% of salary (basic plus DA). Under the old regime, it is allowed up to 10% of salary for private-sector employees (14% for central government employees). Asking your employer to restructure part of your CTC as employer NPS contribution is one of the few tax-saving options that works in the new regime.

## NPS on the payslip
An "NPS" deduction on the payslip is usually your own contribution. The employer's contribution normally appears in the CTC breakup rather than as a deduction, because it is paid in addition to your salary.
