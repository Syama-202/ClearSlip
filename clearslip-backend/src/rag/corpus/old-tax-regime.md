## Tax slabs in the old regime
Under the old tax regime, an individual below 60 years pays: up to ₹2,50,000 nil; ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 at 5%; ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 at 20%; and above ₹10,00,000 at 30%. A 4% health and education cess is added to the tax. Senior citizens have a higher basic exemption.

## Standard deduction in the old regime
Salaried employees get a standard deduction of ₹50,000 under the old regime, compared with ₹75,000 under the new regime.

## Rebate under section 87A in the old regime
In the old regime, a resident individual with taxable income up to ₹5,00,000 gets a rebate of up to ₹12,500 under section 87A, so the tax becomes nil. Above ₹5,00,000 there is no rebate and no marginal relief.

## Deductions available only in the old regime
The old regime allows many exemptions and deductions that the new regime does not: HRA exemption (section 10(13A)), LTA exemption (section 10(5)), professional tax (section 16(iii)), section 80C investments up to ₹1,50,000, section 80CCD(1B) NPS up to ₹50,000, section 80D health insurance, interest on a home loan for a self-occupied house up to ₹2,00,000 (section 24(b)), and others. These deductions reduce taxable income before the slab rates are applied.

## When the old regime can still be better
The old regime has higher slab rates, so it is usually better only for people who claim large deductions — for example a combination of HRA exemption, full 80C, NPS under 80CCD(1B), health insurance and home-loan interest. For most first-time salaried employees with few deductions, the new regime results in lower tax. Comparing both regimes with your actual numbers is the only reliable way to decide.
